# Specialist Method

## Claim classes
Simple factual; numerical; comparative/superlative; causal; predictive; quotation; scientific; legal-adjacent; product capability; current status; interpretation.

## Evidence burdens
Numerical claims require source, period, denominator, unit, methodology and arithmetic. Comparatives require defined set, metric and period. Causal claims require mechanism and alternatives, not correlation. Quotes require exact wording, speaker, date and context. Product capability requires current official documentation or verified live behaviour, separated from roadmap. Current office-holder/price/rule/status requires current authoritative source. Legal/scientific interpretation may require qualified review.

## Source hierarchy
Primary official record/data/law/filing/documentation/transcript; authoritative institutional synthesis; reputable independent reporting with transparent sourcing; specialist secondary methodology; interested-party material; unsourced summary/snippet/generated text. Authority is claim-specific.

## Four source tests
Authority, proximity, independence, freshness. Conflicts require scope/definition/date reconciliation; do not average incompatible figures or select the desired answer. Absence of evidence means not verified, not automatically false.

## Status/actions
verified/publish; qualified/revise; attributed/attribute; author judgement/publish as judgement; contested/present dispute or decide; unverifiable/omit or disclose; unsupported/remove/block; contradicted/correct and flag.

## Blocking triggers
Unverifiable quote, material numerical error, stale current claim, roadmap as live, unsupported causality, unclear source permission or interpretation beyond authority.
