# Release Decision Matrix

## Purpose

Use this guide to make a reproducible release decision against the actual final version. The Gate does not repair missing work. It determines whether required evidence, authority and approval are present.

## 1. Mandatory artefacts by workflow level

### Lightweight

- assignment or instruction;
- final version;
- surface check;
- explicit user action if sending or publishing is requested.

### Standard

- piece direction or brief sufficient for the task;
- active project or voice constraints where applicable;
- final version;
- unresolved-issue record;
- targeted verification when material claims exist.

### Full

- route declaration;
- project manifest where governed;
- editorial brief;
- source pack and Claim Registry where substantive claims were developed;
- relevant argument or anti-slop findings and resolution status;
- edit record for material changes;
- verification record covering the release candidate;
- approval record;
- final version.

### Extended

All full-level artefacts plus:

- versioned source inventory;
- continuity or manuscript record;
- change log;
- provenance or disclosure record;
- waiver record where applicable;
- explicit release owner.

## 2. Artefact validity

For each artefact record:

- present, missing or not required;
- version;
- required status;
- whether stale;
- reason;
- relationship to final draft version.

An artefact may be present but unusable because it covers an earlier draft or a changed claim set.

## 3. Ordered checks

1. **Authority**: route, permissions and release owner are valid.
2. **Factual integrity**: material claims, quotations and attribution are covered.
3. **Human meaning**: authorised thesis, judgement and uncertainty are preserved.
4. **Privacy and disclosure**: source permission, confidentiality, provenance and disclosure duties are satisfied.
5. **Argument and structure**: no unresolved material logic or decision defect remains.
6. **Voice and form**: output fits the intended form and preserves protected authorship.
7. **Surface quality**: no placeholder, tool residue, malformed formatting or production defect remains.
8. **Approval**: required human approval or waiver is explicit and current.

## 4. Outcome matrix

### Ready

Use only when:

- all mandatory artefacts are present and current;
- checks pass or are not applicable;
- no blocker or release condition remains;
- verification is complete or not required;
- disclosure is complete or not required;
- approval is approved or not required.

### Conditional

Use when:

- the core argument and claim set are stable;
- remaining actions are specific and limited;
- conditions have owners and required actions;
- release will not occur until conditions are completed;
- no unresolved integrity blocker remains.

Conditional does not mean “publish now with a note”.

### Human decision required

Use when:

- evidence and policy permit more than one accountable framing;
- a waiver decision is required;
- residual risk is accepted or rejected by an authorised human;
- the Gate cannot choose without becoming the author or executive owner.

### Blocked

Use when:

- a required artefact is missing or stale;
- critical claim is unsupported, contradicted or unverifiable;
- source permission or privacy fails;
- required approval is absent;
- disclosure obligation is unmet and cannot be a limited condition;
- unresolved P0 or material P1 remains;
- the final output contains tool residue or unusable production defects.

## 5. Blocker versus condition

A blocker prevents release and normally requires upstream rework.

A condition is a finite action that does not change the core thesis, claim set or architecture.

Examples:

- missing approved disclosure text: condition when wording is authorised and insertion is mechanical;
- unsupported market-leadership claim: blocker;
- two evidence-consistent strategic framings: human decision;
- typo in a non-material caption: condition or local pre-release correction, depending on workflow.

## 6. Waivers

A waiver must record:

- exact condition waived;
- authorising person;
- reason;
- risk accepted;
- scope and expiry;
- whether law, safety, privacy or truth constraints prohibit waiver.

A waiver cannot make a false claim true or authorise disclosure the owner has no right to make.

## 7. Return paths

Map each issue to the authority that can resolve it:

- missing or unauthorised substance -> Authorship Capture or human author;
- brief conflict -> Editorial Brief Compiler or project owner;
- structural defect -> Argument Reviewer, then Editor;
- voice or local prose defect -> Voice Preserving Editor;
- factual, quotation or current-status defect -> Factual Verifier;
- accountable risk or framing -> human approver.

The Gate does not write the repair itself.

## 8. Stale-decision rules

A Gate decision becomes stale after:

- any material textual edit;
- new or changed material claim;
- changed source or verification status;
- changed release surface or audience;
- changed manifest, approval or disclosure context;
- expired current-as-of evidence.

A cosmetic correction may be handled under a documented local policy, but the final released version must remain identifiable.

## 9. Reproducibility test

Another reviewer should be able to reproduce the outcome from:

- final draft version;
- artefact inventory;
- check evidence;
- blockers and conditions;
- approval and waiver records;
- return paths.

If the decision depends on hidden conversation or intuition, the Gate record is incomplete.
