# Specialist Method

## Scope and authority
The Builder describes repeatable behaviour and editing boundaries. It does not copy signature phrases, imitate a living author closely, infer personality, or treat subject matter as voice. It may issue a provisional profile when evidence is limited.

## Required inputs and intake
1. Confirm profile owner, permission and intended uses.
2. Record each sample's form, date, audience, authorship confidence and editing history.
3. Exclude or downgrade samples with unknown ghostwriting, heavy editing or generated provenance.
4. Ensure the sample set covers the registers the profile is expected to govern.
5. Record accepted and rejected edits as high-value evidence of boundaries.

## Execution procedure
1. Assess sample sufficiency before extracting traits.
2. Analyse six layers: rhetorical development, syntax and cadence, lexical abstraction and terminology, structural sequencing and repetition, punctuation behaviour, and epistemic stance.
3. Extract candidate traits as observable decisions, not adjectives.
4. Cite at least two independent samples for an enduring trait.
5. Search deliberately for counterevidence and outliers.
6. Compare the behaviour with a generic prose baseline to test whether it is distinctive or merely conventional.
7. Separate enduring, register-specific, context-dependent and candidate traits.
8. Translate each accepted trait into a positive writing instruction, a preservation rule and an anti-pattern.
9. Identify protected characteristics and the cost of removing them.
10. Set imitation boundaries and update rules.

## Decision logic
Sample sufficiency defaults:
- provisional: at least three substantial samples or about 1,500 words with known authorship;
- adequate personal: at least six samples across two contexts;
- strong personal: multiple periods, forms and accepted/rejected edits;
- institutional: at least eight approved samples with governance provenance;
- literary: enough material to observe sentence, section and chapter behaviour.

Trait status:
- repeated across registers: enduring;
- repeated within one register: register-specific;
- dependent on topic, audience or form: context-dependent;
- supported once or contradicted equally: candidate or rejected;
- dependent on copied phrases: imitation risk, not a voice rule.

Confidence should reflect sample quality, independence, recurrence, counterevidence and scope. Do not use a numerical confidence without a short evidence rationale.

## Version and staleness
Record the sample-set and permission-record versions in `input_versions`. The profile becomes stale when sample provenance, editing history, inclusion decisions, accepted human edits or register coverage changes materially.

## Stop, escalation and re-entry
Stop when permission is absent, the sample owner is disputed, all samples have unknown editing history or the request seeks close imitation of a living public author.

Escalate provenance questions to the project owner, imitation concerns to the accountable author and form-specific execution to the Brief Compiler or Editor.

Re-enter when confirmed human samples, explicit accepted edits or a new register materially changes evidence. Never auto-learn from generated output merely because it was published.

## Failure modes
- “Authoritative, warm and clear” is not operational.
- A recurring technical noun may reflect domain necessity, not voice.
- Social fragments do not prove fragments belong in board reports.
- Favourite phrases encourage mimicry and brittle output.
- Averaging contradictory registers creates a voice no one actually uses.

## Examples and counterexamples
Valid: “States the governing claim early, then narrows scope before implications” with evidence from three reports.
Invalid: “Uses sophisticated language” without behavioural evidence.
Valid: protect deliberate fragments in founder social posts while excluding them from formal legal-adjacent material.
Invalid: copy memorable wording from samples into new work.

## Output assembly
Return a profile conforming to `references/output-contract.yaml`, including the sample inventory and inclusion decisions, sample assessment, evidenced traits, counterevidence, register profiles, protected and discouraged characteristics, imitation boundaries, confidence and unresolved questions.
