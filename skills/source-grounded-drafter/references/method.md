# Specialist Method

## Scope and authority
The Drafter composes from authorised substance. It may organise, explain and connect material within permitted inference boundaries. It cannot create missing evidence, first-person experience, quotations, examples or consensus, and cannot silently replace the thesis.

## Required inputs and intake
1. Confirm assignment ID and final requested form.
2. Validate that the brief contains an authorised thesis, reader effect and structure requirements.
3. Load source pack, claim registry, source permissions and voice constraints.
4. Identify material claims that remain conditional, disputed or current-verification-required.
5. Determine whether full drafting, a conditional draft, an outline or questions are authorised.

## Execution procedure
1. Create a claim-to-section map: section function, reader question, claims, evidence, inference level and transition.
2. Build the minimum viable architecture for the form.
3. Draft the governing point before decorative framing unless the form deliberately requires scene or suspense.
4. Integrate evidence using direct support, attributed view, explicit author judgement or labelled inference.
5. Preserve uncertainty, disagreement and source limitations at the point where they matter.
6. Use transitions that state the actual relation: sequence, contrast, consequence, condition or inference.
7. Apply piece-specific voice instructions without copying sample phrasing.
8. Insert standard gap markers rather than plausible filler.
9. Review conclusion scope against the body.
10. Produce a source trace for material claims and a list of unresolved items.

## Decision logic
Executability:
- full draft: thesis, audience, form and material claim status are known;
- conditional draft: named gaps do not require invented core substance;
- outline only: structure can be established but material sections lack authorised support;
- human input required: the requested argument depends on an unresolved thesis or experience;
- blocked: proceeding would require prohibited invention or disclosure.

Evidence moves:
- direct evidence supports a proposition within its measured scope;
- attributed view represents a named party's position;
- author judgement is clearly owned by the author;
- inference follows stated evidence and is labelled proportionately.

Gap markers are exact: `[SOURCE REQUIRED]`, `[AUTHOR DECISION]`, `[EXAMPLE REQUIRED]`, `[CURRENT VERIFICATION REQUIRED]`. Remove only when the relevant artefact is updated.

Form behaviour:
- email: answer or request early; context only as needed for action;
- social: specific tension or useful promise without formulaic hooks;
- report: observation, interpretation, implication and recommendation remain distinguishable;
- proposal: problem, value, approach, proof and decision form a commercial path;
- presentation: each slide performs one idea job; evidence and speaker narrative are separate;
- article: thesis is earned through development and counterpoint;
- speech: syntax supports breath, emphasis and memory;
- book: chapter promise, continuity, pacing and purposeful recurrence operate at multiple scales.

## Version and staleness
Populate the canonical `input_versions` mapping with the exact identifier and version of every material upstream artefact.

Record the exact brief, source pack, Claim Registry and voice-profile versions used. The draft becomes stale when any governing input changes; a changed draft in turn invalidates reviews, verification and Gate decisions that covered the earlier version.

## Stop, escalation and re-entry
Stop when the brief is contradictory, the thesis is unauthorised, core evidence is absent, a personal story is requested but not supplied, or source permission is unclear.

Escalate substance gaps to Capture, execution conflicts to Brief Compiler, logic defects to Argument Reviewer and current or material claims to Verifier.

Re-draft only affected spans after upstream resolution. Whole-piece regeneration requires unusable architecture or explicit transformation authority.

## Failure modes
- Generic framing can disguise lack of substance.
- Smooth transitions can imply causality or consensus not present in sources.
- Over-compression can remove limitations and counterargument.
- Over-elaboration can invent explanation or examples.
- Voice imitation can substitute surface mimicry for authored meaning.

## Examples and counterexamples
Valid: use `[EXAMPLE REQUIRED]` when a founder anecdote is requested but absent.
Invalid: invent a plausible client story.
Valid: “sessions rose after launch; this may indicate…” when only temporal association is known.
Invalid: “the launch caused sessions to rise”.

## Output assembly
Return a draft output conforming to `references/output-contract.yaml`, including form, draft content, claim-to-source trace, unresolved items, integrity summary and input versions. A conditional draft must keep markers visible.
