# Form Drafting Playbooks

## Purpose

Use this guide after the brief is executable. It explains how authorised substance should behave in different forms without converting the form into a rigid template.

## 1. Claim-to-section map

Before prose, map:

- unit or section ID;
- function;
- reader question;
- authorised claim IDs;
- evidence references;
- evidence move;
- permitted inference;
- transition relation;
- unresolved marker if any.

Do not draft a section whose function or claim basis cannot be stated.

## 2. Evidence moves

### Direct evidence

State only what the source measures or records. Preserve period, denominator, scope and limitation.

### Attributed view

Name the holder of the view and avoid grammatical constructions that turn attribution into fact.

### Author judgement

Own the judgement explicitly when the form permits. In institutional work, use the authorised organisational stance rather than simulated objectivity.

### Labelled inference

Make the reasoning step visible with proportionate language: suggests, indicates, is consistent with, may mean, or supports the interpretation that.

## 3. Transition discipline

Choose the actual relation:

- sequence: then, subsequently;
- contrast: however, by contrast;
- condition: if, unless, provided that;
- consequence: therefore, as a result, only when earned;
- elaboration: specifically, for example;
- inference: this suggests, one implication is;
- concession: although, despite.

A smooth transition that implies an unsupported relation is an integrity defect.

## 4. Paragraph construction

A functional paragraph normally contains:

- a job or proposition;
- development or evidence;
- limitation, implication or transition where needed.

Do not force every paragraph into the same shape. Vary length and rhythm according to argumentative work and voice.

## 5. Email and message

- Put the answer, request or decision before nonessential history.
- State constraints directly without manufacturing blame.
- Make owner and next action visible.
- Preserve relational warmth through specificity, not generic pleasantries.
- Do not bury refusal or bad news in ambiguous optimism.

## 6. Social and marketing content

- Begin with a specific tension, observation or useful promise grounded in the source pack.
- Avoid formulaic “in today’s world”, exaggerated transformation and universal claims.
- Use one primary idea and one reader action.
- Preserve platform rhythm without fragmenting every sentence mechanically.
- Do not invent customer examples, performance figures or founder experience.

## 7. Report

Use a visible evidence-to-decision chain:

1. observation;
2. interpretation;
3. implication;
4. recommendation;
5. owner, assumption and next action.

Label judgement where readers may otherwise mistake it for measurement. Put methodological limits near the claim they constrain.

## 8. Proposal

- Establish the problem independently of the proposed solution.
- Explain the mechanism by which the approach creates value.
- Use proof at the point of doubt, not in a detached credibility section only.
- Make scope, exclusions, dependencies and commercial next step explicit.
- Avoid fabricated inevitability or false scarcity.

## 9. Presentation

For each slide:

- write the idea job in one sentence;
- state one governing claim;
- select the minimum evidence required on-slide;
- move detail to notes or appendix without removing decision-critical qualification;
- define the visual relationship, not merely a decorative image;
- ensure the sequence produces narrative movement.

Slide titles should advance the argument where appropriate, not merely label topics.

## 10. Article or essay

- Establish a question or tension worth resolving.
- Earn the thesis through evidence, reasoning, scene or counterpoint as the form requires.
- Keep examples connected to the claim they illuminate.
- Represent credible alternatives proportionately.
- Let the conclusion complete the developed argument rather than introduce a stronger claim.

## 11. Speech or script

- Read aloud during drafting.
- Use sentence length, pauses and recurrence for breath and memory.
- Place technical qualifiers where they remain audible.
- Avoid nested clauses that depend on rereading.
- Distinguish spoken emphasis from typographic emphasis.

## 12. Book chapter

Work at three scales:

- sentence and paragraph movement;
- section architecture;
- contribution to the whole manuscript.

Track concepts introduced, recalled and deferred. Preserve purposeful motifs and recurrence. Avoid summarising the whole book in every chapter.

## 13. Gap markers

Use exactly:

- `[SOURCE REQUIRED]`;
- `[AUTHOR DECISION]`;
- `[EXAMPLE REQUIRED]`;
- `[CURRENT VERIFICATION REQUIRED]`.

Each marker must have a structured gap record with owner and location. Do not replace markers with plausible filler. Remove a marker only after the relevant upstream artefact changes.

## 14. Source trace

Trace every material claim by location, claim ID, source reference and evidence move. A location may contain multiple claims. A claim may appear in multiple locations.

`claim_trace_complete: true` requires:

- no unresolved gap markers;
- every material claim traced;
- every referenced source authorised;
- conclusion scope covered by the developed claims.

## 15. Regeneration threshold

Regenerate a whole piece only when:

- the architecture cannot perform the brief;
- the governing thesis changes;
- the form changes materially;
- local repair would create more semantic risk than reconstruction;
- explicit transformation authority is granted.

Otherwise revise locally and preserve stable authored work.
