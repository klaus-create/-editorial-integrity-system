# Specialist Method

## Elicitation sequence
1. What must the reader understand?
2. What happened or was observed?
3. Which parts are fact, judgement, recollection or hypothesis?
4. What evidence would a sceptical reader ask for?
5. Where does the author disagree with the conventional framing?
6. What must not be softened, exaggerated or invented?
7. What remains uncertain or contested?
8. What should the reader think, feel or do?

## Atomisation
Split compound claims. “The launch failed because targeting changed” becomes performance result, targeting change, timing relation and causal proposition, each with separate evidence status.

## Epistemic classes
verified fact; supplied-source claim; attributed view; author judgement; personal recollection; inference; contested; uncertain; unsupported. Confidence is separate from class.

## Sufficiency
Sufficient only when thesis is authorised or explicitly unresolved, material claims have status, evidence duties and missing inputs are visible, reader effect is known, protected language is recorded and no unresolved contradiction silently changes the piece. Conditional sufficiency may permit outlining but not publication drafting.

## Stop and escalation
Competing theses, disputed attribution, unclear confidential-source permission, missing first-person experience or unsupported high-consequence claims require human input. Current claims go to the Verifier; voice inference goes to Voice Profile Builder.
